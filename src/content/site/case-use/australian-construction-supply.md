An Australian construction supply distributor wanted to extend its fastener-and-power-tool catalog into workholding for the trades its branches serve daily: plumbers, electricians, and site fabricators. The gap in its range was portable pipe workholding — a product its branch staff kept being asked for and couldn't supply.

## Finding the right product

The requirement was specific. Trades work at height, in tight spaces, and on uneven ground, so the vise had to be portable, sturdy, and capable of holding pipe securely in any orientation. Stavalk's pipe tripod vise — a cast iron head with integrated pipe jaws for threading and cutting, mounted on a folding three-leg tripod — matched the brief without modification to the core design.

## Specifying for Australian conditions

The distributor specified HT200 cast iron heads to keep the unit portable, with hardened jaw inserts rated for repeated pipe gripping in carbon and galvanised steel. Because the product would be used on active construction sites, anti-corrosion requirements were stricter than average: the finish was specified as powder coat on the head plus zinc plating on the tripod hardware, and we ran the 48-hour salt spray test as part of the project qualification.

## Certification and documentation

Australian construction supply carries an appetite for compliance paperwork. The project shipped with the full documentation set — material certificates, test reports for the jaw hardness and clamping force, CE marking documentation, and ISO 9001 process records. The distributor's compliance team reviewed and accepted the package before the first container left Qingdao, which kept the importing and stocking decision entirely within its own planning window.

## Result and reorder

The first order covered pipe tripod vises in two jaw capacity ranges and became a stocked line in the distributor's central warehouse within a quarter. The line has been reordered on the cycle since, and the distributor has since asked Stavalk to quote a complementary range of stationary bench vises for the same branch network — evidence of a product sourcing relationship that grew into a broader range conversation.
