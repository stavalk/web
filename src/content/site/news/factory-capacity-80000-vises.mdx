Capacity matters in workholding. A factory that cannot commit to your order volume on schedule is not really a supplier — it is a risk. This article looks at the concrete numbers behind Stavalk's production capacity and what they mean for OEM buyers planning their 2026 and 2027 programs.

## The plant at a glance

Stavalk's bench vise production is concentrated in a single 8,000 square meter facility in Qingdao, Shandong Province. The plant is organized around the process flow of a vise: sand casting and foundry operations, machining, heat treatment, surface finishing, and assembly. Keeping the full process under one roof is deliberate — it lets us control quality at every step instead of coordinating with outside workshops.

## The workforce

More than 200 workers operate across the plant, split between foundry, machining, and assembly sections. Skilled machinists run the CNC and conventional equipment, foundry teams manage molding and pouring, and the assembly lines handle fitting, adjustment, and final inspection. Supervisors on each line are responsible for process documentation, so the production record for every batch — from melt chemistry to final torque check — is complete and auditable.

## Annual capacity and order scheduling

The plant has an annual capacity of 80,000+ bench vises. This is real, planned capacity, not a theoretical figure: it is based on the number of molding lines, machining cells, and assembly stations operating in two shifts, measured against confirmed order history. We publish a quarterly booking calendar, and confirmed production slots are allocated in the order they are locked with a deposit.

## Export reach

More than 50 countries receive Stavalk bench vises. The main markets are Europe (Germany, France, the Netherlands, Poland, the UK), North America (United States, Canada, Mexico), and growing volumes in South America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, and Australia. CE marking, ISO 9001, ISO 14001, and RoHS documentation support these export programs, and we have handled container-level orders to every one of these regions.

## Flexibility for OEM customers

Capacity is not just a number — it is about how orders flex around it. OEM customers occupy part of the plant's annual capacity through framework orders, reserving production slots for their branded lines. Within those slots, we handle model mix changes, custom finishes, and private-label branding without rebuilding the schedule. This is how we sustain 50–200 piece MOQs on standard models while still serving larger distributors with multi-container orders.

## Capacity planning for buyers

For buyers, the practical question is simple: is the factory able to commit to the volumes you actually need, in the weeks you need them? Before you sign a first order, ask for the current booking calendar and confirm available production slots for your target delivery window. A factory that shows you its real calendar — including what is already booked — is one that can be trusted with a second order.
