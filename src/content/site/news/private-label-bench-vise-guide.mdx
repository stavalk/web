Private-label bench vises are good business. Your brand already trusts you with its workshop; a branded six-inch cast iron vise on the rack converts that trust into a margin-rich SKU. What trips up first-time private-label programs is usually not the factory — it is a fuzzy specification. This article walks through the decisions we walk through with hardware brands every week.

## The brand story starts with the casting

The most important branding decision is how your name appears on the vise. Two practical options:

- **Logotype cast into the body.** The name is raised or recessed into the casting itself. It looks premium, lasts as long as the vise, and is the choice of brands that expect to sell the same model for years.
- **Logo painted or laser-marked on the finished surface.** Cheaper to change between runs, ideal for testing a market before you commit to a dedicated pattern.

Cast-in logos require a dedicated pattern, which carries a one-time tooling cost. We recommend cast-in logos for any SKU you plan to sell beyond a single season, and painted or laser markings for trial runs.

## Choosing your colorway

A bench vise is mostly body color, so the paint matters more than on almost any other tool. Greys, blacks, and machine greens dominate the professional market; a single bright accent — red, orange, or acid green — on the handle or jaws differentiates a consumer-targeted line. We offer the standard Stavalk finishes in a range of RAL colors, and custom colorways are fully supported. Tell us the retail context and we will help you pick a color that photographs well on a shelf.

## Packaging that protects and sells

Professional vises are heavy, so packaging is first a structural problem. Each vise is secured in a fit-for-purpose insert with the handle and pipe jaw parts nested, then cartoned with the correct grammage for the shipment method. Retail-grade cartons add printed branding, product imagery, and specification callouts on the box. For brands selling through e-commerce marketplaces, we also prepare barcoded, export-ready cartons with the correct GTINs applied.

## Specification and certification

A private-label program needs its spec locked in writing before the pattern work starts. Jaw width, clamping force, material grade (HT200, HT250, or 45# forged steel), jaw hardness range, and the finish codes should all be stated on the final project spec. Depending on your target markets, CE marking, RoHS, and REACH documentation are prepared at the same time, so there are no surprises when your distributor asks for the paperwork.

## The path from idea to container

A typical private-label project runs in four stages. First, specification freeze and sample approval — 7–14 days, including your cast-in logo if the pattern is new. Second, pattern and tooling manufacture where needed. Third, a production run of 25–35 days with pre-shipment inspection. Fourth, consolidation and export. We handle the complete commercial process including commercial invoice, packing list, and all export documentation from Qingdao.

## Typical MOQ for private label

Private-label runs follow our standard MOQs — 50–200 pieces for standard cast iron models, and 20–50 pieces for heavy or specialty vises. The cast-in logo pattern is a one-time cost that ammortises quickly across even a small first run. For brands testing a new market, we recommend pairing a standard colorway with a painted logo for the first order, then upgrading to a cast-in logo pattern once the SKU proves itself.
