Selecting the right material for a bench vise is one of the most consequential decisions an OEM buyer or procurement manager makes. The material determines vibration damping, clamping force ceiling, fatigue life, and ultimately, how the vise performs in the hands of the end user. This article compares the two dominant bench vise materials — grey cast iron and 45# forged steel — and explains when each is the right choice.

## Grey cast iron: the workhorse of bench vises

Grey cast iron, specifically the HT200 and HT250 grades, accounts for the majority of bench vise bodies worldwide. HT200 offers a tensile strength of approximately 200 MPa, while HT250 reaches 250 MPa. Both grades provide excellent vibration damping — a critical property for machining and assembly work where chatter ruins surface finish.

Cast iron also machines well. After sand casting, the body can be milled, drilled, and tapped to precise dimensions with relatively low tooling wear. For high-volume production, this translates directly into lower per-unit manufacturing costs. The material's natural graphite flake structure provides self-lubricating properties at the slide surfaces, reducing friction during jaw adjustment.

## 45# forged steel: built for impact

45# forged steel (a medium-carbon steel with approximately 0.45% carbon) delivers significantly higher tensile strength — typically 600–700 MPa after heat treatment — compared to grey cast iron. This makes forged steel the preferred material for vises used in heavy fabrication, structural steel work, and environments where the vise may be subjected to hammering, prying, or impact loads.

The trade-off is weight and cost. A forged steel vise body of equivalent jaw width will be heavier than its cast iron counterpart, and the forging plus heat treatment process adds to per-unit cost. Steel also lacks the vibration damping properties of cast iron, so it transmits more shock to the mounting surface.

## When to specify cast iron

Grey cast iron is the right choice for most general-purpose workshop applications: woodworking, light metal fabrication, educational settings, and hobbyist benches. It offers the best balance of cost, weight, and performance for these use cases. HT250 is preferred for vises with jaw widths of 6 inches and above, where the higher tensile strength provides additional safety margin.

## When to specify forged steel

Forged steel vises suit industrial environments with demanding duty cycles: shipyards, structural steel fabrication, mining equipment repair, and any application where the vise will be hit with a hammer or used as a makeshift anvil. The higher impact resistance and fatigue strength mean a forged steel vise will outlast a cast iron vise under severe abuse, though both materials perform identically under normal workshop conditions.

## Making the call for your product line

Most OEM buyers benefit from offering both materials across their product range. A cast iron line covers the high-volume, cost-sensitive segments, while a forged steel premium tier captures industrial buyers willing to pay more for durability. The key is matching material selection to the application profile of each target market.
